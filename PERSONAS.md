# Bard Beats, Personas

Personas for prioritizing the build. The main persona is the person making loops in short bursts at home on the 3DS, jamming until a pattern clicks and ending up with a song. Flavor is a requirement, not decoration: the app should feel like a bard's lute, not a DAW.

## Main persona: The Couch Bard

- Who: Someone making music in short bursts at home on the 3DS, squeezed between everything else. They pick up the handheld, jam for a few minutes, and want to walk away with a loop or a song, not a homework assignment.
- What they need: the loop first. A grid that is fast to tap, live play that feels like an instrument, and a record flow that catches the pattern when it clicks. Undo when it doesn't. The song mode has to be as simple as chaining clips, and the vocal layer has to be one tap to record.
- What they don't need: a DAW. No mixer, no plugin browser, no piano roll with a hundred lanes, no theory test before they can play a note. The app is a loop maker, not a studio.
- How the app serves them: the loop is the home screen. Jam, record, save, copy, chain. Everything is built for thumbs and a stylus while the loop is running, with starting, stopping, recording, and undoing always one tap away.

## Secondary persona: The Shower Singer

- Who: Someone who hums melodies all day and wants to catch them. They sing over everything and want their voice in the song, not just instruments.
- What they need: vocals that are easy to lay down, per clip or over the whole song, with the mic they already have. The vocal is a layer, so a bad take never ruins the loop underneath.
- What they don't need: studio quality. The 3DS mic is sketchpad-level and they know it. They want the sketch captured, not a polished take.
- How the app serves them: one-tap vocal recording, per clip and over the song, with retakes that cost nothing.

## Secondary persona: The Chiptune Tinkerer

- Who: Someone who loves retro game sounds and wants to make loops that sound like the games they grew up on. They know what a step sequencer is and they want the 3DS to sound like itself.
- What they need: the grid to be the star, a good set of lo-fi and chiptune-flavored instruments, and loops they can save and build on.
- What they don't need: realistic orchestral samples or a preset library the size of a store. A few great sounds beat a hundred okay ones.
- How the app serves them: the grid view front and center, and launch instruments chosen for character, not count.

## Secondary persona: The New Music Maker

- Who: Someone who has never made music and doesn't read sheet music. They want to make something that sounds good without a lesson first.
- What they need: the app to be forgiving and fast. Tap the grid, hear it loop, fix the note that sounds wrong, keep going. Live play that can't really go wrong.
- What they don't need: depth they can't use yet. The app should not bury them in tracks, effects, or settings on day one.
- How the app serves them: 16 steps, a handful of tracks, and a record flow that only keeps what they like.

## Secondary persona: The Spellbook Bard

- Who: A D&D player who wants music for their character, a short loop for each spell, played at the table when they cast it. Like a prepared spellbook, but the pages are loops.
- What they need: short loops, saved by name, playable on cue with one tap. A loop for Fireball, a loop for Healing Word, a loop for the big moment. They make the loop at home and play it at the table.
- What they don't need: full songs or long arrangements. A spell cue is seconds long. They don't need song mode for this, they need a shelf of named loops they can reach fast.
- How the app serves them: loops saved with names, a quick play list, one tap to play. The loop list is their spellbook.

## Tentative persona: The Young Bard

- Who: A kid making loops on the 3DS for fun, maybe for their own games or just to hear something they made.
- What they need: the same fast loop and a low price of failure. Nothing in the app should punish a wrong tap, and a bad loop should be one tap from gone.
- Status: tentative. Not a design driver until confirmed.

## Anti-persona: The DAW Purist

- Who: A producer who believes real music needs a full DAW, a PC, plugins, and a proper mix. They see a handheld loop maker as a toy.
- What this means for the app: do not try to convert them. The app wins by being fast and fun, so it never becomes the thing that takes twenty minutes of setup before a single note. If the app ever makes someone read a manual before they can make a loop, it has failed its own main persona too.

## Prioritization rule

- The Couch Bard decides. When a feature helps them make a loop in a short burst, it goes first. When it helps someone else but slows them down, it waits.
- The loop first, then the song, then the voice. That order comes from how they actually play: jam a loop, chain it into a song, sing over it.
- Named loops with one-tap playback serve the Spellbook Bard and the Couch Bard alike. A saved loop you can play on cue is the same feature, used two ways.
